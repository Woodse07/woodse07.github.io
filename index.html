About

I’m a Senior Software Engineer and Tech Lead at Qualtrics in Dublin. I work on the Partner Accelerator team, building solutions that cut the time it takes partners to get value out of the platform. Before that I was on Distributions, working on interactive survey taking across channels and the services behind it.

I hold a Masters in Computer Science from Trinity College Dublin. My dissertation, Understanding Emotion with Multimodal Machine Learning, trained models to read emotion from facial movement, gesture and voice together, using recordings of real conversations rather than posed expressions. Modelling how signals changed over time beat reading them frame by frame, more features beat fewer, and audio and visual together over time was the most accurate combination I tested.

Outside work I cycle and run, which is where a few of the 3D models started — a bike mount among them. The rest is generative sketches and parametric design, some of which is below.

Projects

Things running on a server at home, built because I wanted them to exist.

  • Home automation

    A Home Assistant install I keep extending: 48 automations across seven areas of the house, with most sensors on Zigbee through zigbee2mqtt. It tracks energy use, temperature and humidity room by room, drives lighting and blinds, and handles camera detections from Frigate. Presence, doorbell events and the state of the house all feed the same set of rules.

  • Departure board

    A small FastAPI service in Docker, built like the dot-matrix boards at Dublin Heuston. It lists the trains passing in the next while — departing west or arriving east — with scheduled and expected times and how late each one is running, served as JSON.

Sketches

Small generative pieces, written in p5.js and running live in the browser.

  • Random Walk

    A circle randomly chooses a direction — up, down, left or right — every frame. Each time it passes through a cell in the grid that cell’s opacity increases, so the path it has taken slowly emerges.

    It’s currently running as the background of this page.

  • Infinite Unique Snowflake Generator

    Algorithmic randomness generates a different snowflake on every run — reuse the same seed and you get the same flake back. Complexity, branching and size are exposed as parameters, and the output is tuned to print support-free even at fractal-fine tips.

  • Cube Shelf for SKÅDIS

    Storage that adds pegboard rather than consuming it — both side panels carry the SKÅDIS slot spacing, so the grid continues straight through the shelf and hooks still mount to it. Prints as a single piece.

  • Organic Shelf Bracket

    Designed with topology optimisation: the solver strips material from everywhere the load path doesn’t travel, leaving a bone-like form that is shaped by the forces rather than drawn. Holds a 10kg shelf in PLA+ at 25% infill.

  • Parametric LEGO frames and shelves

    A family of SKÅDIS display pieces driven from a handful of variables, so one source generates every size in the set rather than each being modelled separately.
