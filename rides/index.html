How it works

Every line is a ride I actually rode — 212 of them, recorded on a Garmin and pulled out of Strava, a little over 4,584 km between August 2024 and July 2026. Almost all of it is within an hour of Dublin, with the rest picked up in Vancouver, Mallorca, Rimini and Amsterdam.

Nothing counts how many times a road was ridden. Every track is drawn faintly on top of the ones before it, so a road ridden once stays pale and a road ridden two hundred times builds up into a deep burnt red. The lines keep the same thickness however far you zoom in, which is the whole trick: routes that had merged into one dark line separate back into the roads that make it up.

The only thing not drawn from the rides is the land, and it is a shade off the sea rather than a line drawn around it: a shoreline is a line and so is a ride, so the coast and the roads running along it came out as the same mark. It is OpenStreetMap coastline close to where I have ridden and Natural Earth further out, both saved into the page when it was built, so nothing is loaded while you look at it. Turbo sessions are left out, and the streets nearest home are trimmed off each ride before the data ever reaches the page.
