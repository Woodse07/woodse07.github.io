How it works

Every line is a ride I actually rode. There are 212 of them, recorded on a Garmin and pulled out of Strava — a little over 4,584 km between August 2024 and July 2026, almost all of it within an hour of Dublin, with the rest picked up on trips to Vancouver, Mallorca, Rimini and Amsterdam.

Nothing counts how many times a road was ridden. Every track is drawn faintly on top of the ones before it, so a road ridden once stays pale and a road ridden two hundred times builds up into a deep burnt red. The lines keep the same thickness however far you zoom in, which is the whole trick: routes that had merged into one dark line separate back into the roads that make it up.

Almost everything you can see is the tracks themselves — the climbs through the Wicklow Gap, the grid of streets I ride to get out of the city, all of it drawn only from where the bike has been. The one thing that is not is the land, and it is a shade off the sea rather than a line drawn around it. Drawing the coast as a line was a mistake: a shoreline is a line and so is a ride, so a coast road and the coast it runs along came out as the same mark and the two tangled wherever they met. A change of colour says the same thing, and leaves the only lines on the map the ones I rode.

The coast is drawn carefully where I have ridden and roughly everywhere else. Close in it has to be good enough that a seafront lines up with the sea; carrying that everywhere would be an enormous file describing a shape you are looking at from hundreds of miles away. Pull back and you get whole countries rather than the corner of one. Both versions were saved into the page when it was built, so nothing is loaded while you look at it and nothing about your visit goes anywhere.

It is one map rather than five, and like the train map it is drawn by hand rather than by a mapping library. The buttons above only move the camera, so you can drag from Dublin out to the Wicklow Gap, or keep going and find Mallorca where it actually is.

Turbo sessions are left out. Zwift puts you in a world of its own, which lands off the coast of New York, and plotting those would smear a continent I have never cycled across the map.

The rides mostly start from my front door, so the streets nearest home are taken out before the data reaches this page.
